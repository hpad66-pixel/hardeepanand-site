<p class="first">The board packet still opens the same way: capital plan, consent-order status, a digital slide nobody can connect to a pump.</p>
<p>Someone says AI. The room nods. Then we go back to pipes — because pipes are what we believe we are.</p>
<p>I don't argue with the pipes. I argue with the self-concept that stops at the pipe. That self-concept is why we keep buying tools that never change how we operate.</p>

<h2>The seven truths</h2>
<p>McKinsey leads. Water translates. Each truth is a Systems Lens panel — not a checklist to buy against.</p>

<ol>
<li><strong>Teammate, not tool</strong> — AI for the lift station, not the pilot theater. Sense → suggest → decide → measure. Human override stays lit.</li>
<li><strong>Build vs buy</strong> — Buy commodity systems. Build meaning. Route every operating question to a shared catalog and an accountable owner. Shelfware is the coral dead-end.</li>
<li><strong>Tribal knowledge</strong> — Score each truth with a pump-station example. Capture the hidden map before the operator retires.</li>
<li><strong>Design for the swap</strong> — Shared meaning layer over SCADA · GIS · CMMS · CIS. Systems stay replaceable.</li>
<li><strong>Evidence gate</strong> — Supervised pilot → evidence → careful expansion. No autonomy without a named override.</li>
<li><strong>Platform / tasks</strong> — Centralize identity, meaning, access, audit. Decentralize Tuesday.</li>
<li><strong>Adoption flywheel</strong> — Snapshot vs operating curve. If the spend did not bend a vital sign, you bought shelfware.</li>
</ol>

<blockquote class="pullquote"><span class="pq-mark" aria-hidden="true">“</span><p>We have rules and data but not alignment — so pick the battle that fixes the operating system first.</p><cite class="pq-cite"><span class="pq-name">Hardeep Anand</span><span class="pq-publication">The Systems Lens</span></cite></blockquote>
