Seven operating truths for a utility that still thinks it’s only a pipe shop

Hardeep Anand · The Systems Lens · Draft for review · 2026-10-02
Spine: McKinsey — The seven operating truths of AI-native companies (lead) · NextEra / John Ketchum (supporting)

The board packet still opens the same way. Capital plan. Consent-order status. A slide about “digital transformation” that nobody can connect to a pump.

Someone asks whether we should buy another dashboard. Someone else says AI. The room nods. Then we go back to pipes — because pipes are what we believe we are.

I don’t argue with the pipes. I argue with the self-concept that stops at the pipe. That self-concept is why we keep buying tools that never change how we operate.

Aside from power — then back to water. NextEra’s CEO told McKinsey they are a technology company that delivers electricity. Water has not made that flip. We still organize as civil shops with a help desk. The seven truths are an operating system. NextEra is proof a regulated utility can change identity first.

Tools without identity change become shelfware.

McKinsey’s seven operating truths LEAD. Below, each truth is one Systems Lens panel. Read them as one connected system — not a checklist to buy against.

Truth 1AI is not a tool; it’s a teammate

In a plant, a tool is a wrench. A teammate shares context, watches the weak signal, and still yields to the operator when judgment matters.

Put the model next to the operator on a real call at the station. A slide in a conference room will not tell you if the crew can use it.

Take this with youName one decision this quarter where the model sits beside the operator, and the operator still signs.

Truth 2Know what to build and what to buy

You do not need to invent another historian. You do need a shared definition of “critical pump,” “SSO event,” and “verified service line” across GIS, CMMS, and the capital plan. Vendors sell features. Only you own semantics.

Take this with youFor one painful decision this month: what must you own in-house versus what can you buy?

Truth 3Your model isn’t the bottleneck — accessing tribal knowledge is

Every utility has a person who “just knows” which pump surges after a certain storm. When that person retires, the model does not save you. The missing map does.

Test each truth on a pump station. Walk the site. Open the work order. If you cannot point at the pump, you do not have an example yet.

Take this with youPick one rule that only lives in someone’s head. Write it down, name the owner, and put it where the next operator can find it.

Truth 4Design for the swap, not the stack

SCADA, GIS, CMMS, CIS will change. Architecture should assume swap: thin shared meaning on top; best-in-class systems underneath; governance that survives a vendor change.

Take this with youIf one vendor disappeared tomorrow, which shared definitions would still stand?

Truth 5Trust precedes autonomy

Progressive autonomy. The system earns freedom the way a new operator does — under supervision, with evidence, with a human who can override.

Take this with youNo expansion of autonomy without a written evidence gate and a named override.

Truth 6Centralize the platform; decentralize the tasks

What works: shared platform for identity, meaning, access, and audit — while plant and field teams solve Tuesday on top of it. What fails: centralizing every workflow until the people closest to the asset wait for permission to think.

Take this with youName what must be central (identity, meaning, access) and what must stay local (task design, crew judgment).

Truth 7Adoption is a flywheel, not a rollout

If your AI spend never changes overtime, SSO frequency, pump failures, or time-to-locate a record, you do not have adoption. You have a purchase order.

You can have rules and a data warehouse and still have the crew and the capital plan looking at different pictures. Fix that before you buy the next tool.

Take this with youPick one vital sign. Draw the baseline. After the tool lands, ask what changed on that sign.
